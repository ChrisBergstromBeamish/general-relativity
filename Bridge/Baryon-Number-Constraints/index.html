Part of the section Bridge of Physics: Particle Reconstruction and General Relativity.

Abstract. A neutron, a neutral pion and an antineutron all have zero electric charge. Their baryon numbers are \(+1,0,-1\). A charge measurement therefore leaves a physical distinction to be explained. The hadron families specify that distinction more closely. In proton-charge units their readings satisfy \(2(Q-I_3)=B+S+C+B'\), with the evidential status of each multiplet assignment stated below. The published charge interval supplies a standing wave for every charge in the table and for every intermediate value. We derive the condition under which a connection or an admission rule can select the matching readings while keeping the attribute spaces separate. Two published spin counts give different results. A whole state in one winding sector must have \(k\equiv B\pmod 2\) to reproduce measured spin parity. Identifying \(k=B\) in one winding sector fails for helium-3 and helium-4. The complete-packet action count \(n\) is conserved in smooth spin evolution and in each supported analyser outcome under the published laws. Identifying a conserved count with signed baryon number requires its values on independently identified particles, its addition over separated wholes and its preservation on every admitted event branch.
