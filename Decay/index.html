Attributes — Lifetime and the formation of daughters

Abstract. In this theory, decay is the first collapse that leaves one parent as two or more complete particles. Granularity determines the complete outcomes available on each internal space separately. Collapse acts on those spaces again and again as the parent pattern evolves. Each collapse takes place on curved space. On a fixed reference map, the chance of landing in a region is the share of physical volume there, and conservation of that volume under the wave’s evolution selects the square of the wave amplitude. The equal chance of equal volumes and the conservation of volume are hypotheses of the gravity paper, carried here with their labels as LT-P17 and LT-P18. The wave’s evolution is an import, LT-P16. At some point the spaces land far enough apart that a complete set of readings travels with each of two outgoing particles. The first collapse that does this is the decay. The elapsed time on the parent’s clock is its lifetime.

8. Three summaries

For a child. Each of a particle’s inside spaces has its own wave. Granularity gives that wave the things it can become, and Collapse picks an outcome there again and again. The wave curves the space where it can land. Two patches that look the same size on a flat map can hold different amounts of real space. The one that holds more gets more landings. The square of the wave tells us how much chance each patch gets. At some point the pieces land far enough apart that two whole particles travel away. The time until that happens is the lifetime. If none of the things the waves can become is two whole particles, the particle stays one particle for ever.

For a physics teacher. Granularity restricts the outcomes on each internal space separately. Collapse gives the surviving parent repeated opportunities. At each one, the volume of curved space per unit of a fixed reference map is proportional to the squared amplitude of the wave present then. Integrating that density over outcomes in which the spaces land far enough apart to make complete daughters gives \(p\). The first such outcome is the decay. A constant conditional \(p\) leaves \((1-p)^n\) after \(n\) collapses. A constant conditional collapse rate \(r\) gives exponential survival at rate \(rp\). Regular spacing gives steps. Motion and gravity convert between the parent clock and the observer’s clock. A change in the daughter share would also be visible on the parent clock. Where none of the outcomes that Granularity allows is two particles, \(p\) is zero at every collapse and the particle is stable.

For a theoretical physicist. Granularity restricts each internal space’s supported endpoints. Collapse recurs on the participating spaces. Conditional on the surviving history and the next opportunity, the normalized probability-volume element of the current spatial wave is \(\lvert\psi^-\rvert^2dV_0\). The integral over configurations with complete, separating daughters is the mark probability. The first mark in that set turns one parent into two or more particles. Conditional marking gives the geometric count, and constant split intensity gives \(S(u)=e^{-rpu}\). The spatial wave and the external ruler enter the measure and the definition of the daughter set, respectively. The factor \(f/\gamma\) converts the resulting proper-time intensity to reference time. If no supported endpoint has complete, separating daughters, the mark probability vanishes at every collapse and the pattern is stable.

2. Method

  1. Follow one parent. Prepare its separate internal states on one external support. Register the clocks used to compare the active spaces with the parent clock. At each collapse, determine which complete outcomes Granularity allows, how much physical-volume share the ensuing motion has placed on them, and which readings travel together. Stop the clock at the first complete split. This calculates the random variables in LT-O1–O3 and O6.
  2. Use the spin apparatus as a controlled comparison. A held observer prepares one elementary doublet, a top, a positive-energy buffer and a ready apparatus coordinate. At fixed coupling, vary the spin preparation and buffer phase. Integrate the overlap on the laboratory’s proper clock and evaluate both outgoing branches, including recoil. This distinguishes a change of spin from the formation of daughters.
  3. Repeat and transport the parent preparation. Count survivors or corrected daughter tags in successive bins. Compare the survivor fraction and the channel fraction among decays in each bin. For a moving preparation integrate the clock factor along its trajectory. For parents held at two heights, measure the clock ratio independently, compare at equal parent ages, and calculate the split share at each height from its own pre-collapse state. These operations test LT-O2, O4 and O5.
  4. Scan energy separately. Hold the identified transition fixed, specify the coherent signal and detector response, and calculate the measured profile. Reverse the signal’s phase on part of its history while holding survival fixed. This tests the inference from a lifetime to a width in LT-O5.
