# The Observations with High Redshift

From Section 19 of the paper [Reconciling the Schwarzschild Solution with Conservation of Mass-Energy](https://chrisbergstrombeamish.github.io/general-relativity/Reconciling-the-Schwarzschild-Solution-with-Conservation-of-Mass-Energy/), version 1.0.0, word for word. The comparison with the calculation is in the paper's Section 17.

The list comes from a search for the observations of light redshifted by gravity at its source with the highest redshift, ideally above 20 percent; the search was given no account of this paper's physics. Every row is as the search reported it, and not every row has yet been checked at its source. A row is not left out because it is disputed. It is labelled.

The rows are in the order of the observed redshift, highest first. The redshift is $z=E_0/E_{\mathrm{obs}}-1$, from the laboratory energy $E_0$ of the line and the observed energy. The column "Schwarzschild's formula" is $(1-2.95325\,M/R)^{-1/2}-1$, with the mass in suns and the radius in kilometres, with no correction for spin; by Section 4 the conservation derivation gives the same figure from the same mass and radius (Section 17).

**1. Lines from accretion disks around the bodies called black holes.** The shift is from gravity and from the gas's motion together.

|---|---|---|---|
| MCG−6-30-15, XMM-Newton, June 2000 | A broad excess from 3 to 8 keV; iron's line at 6.40 keV with a red wing down to about 3 keV. **Redshift: about 1.13 at the end of the wing; no fitted value there.** | $1.6\pm0.4$ million suns, from the delay and the width of a hydrogen line. The disk fit took the inner edge at 1.23 $G M/c^2$. Schwarzschild's formula: No real value at that radius. That radius is $0.615\,L$, with $L=2GM/c^2$ as this paper defines it for a body at rest: inside $L$. The fit used general relativity's spinning black hole, in which the event horizon is at another radius, and its radius is not the radius of the circles around the body. Whether this is light arriving outside from inside an event horizon, the observation that Section 18 names as the one that decides, takes the calculation with this paper's formulas. That calculation is owed. | The broad profile was seen again with Chandra. No measured split between gravity and motion. Source: Wilms and others 2001, arXiv:astro-ph/0110520. |
| RX J1131−1231, Chandra, 2004 to 2014 | Iron's line at 6.40 keV, lowest fitted energy 3.78 keV in the quasar's frame. **Redshift: 0.69 (+0.05, −0.07).** | 130 million suns, from a hydrogen line's width and the brightness. No single radius; a fit with one radius gave 8.5 $G M/c^2$. Schwarzschild's formula: 0.144 at that radius. | The shifted peaks recur. Later simulations could not give the whole spread of energies. **What the search looked for and did not find.** A surface line of a neutron star above 20 percent whose ion is confirmed and whose mass and radius were measured without the line and without a model of the atmosphere or of the bursts. A measurement that separates gravity from motion for the disk lines or for S2.

**What the list shows.** Where the redshift is small and everything is measured, the calculation and the observation agree: Sirius B, to within the one percent of the measurement. Above 20 percent no observation is yet free of dispute. In the four that have a published mass and radius the line is redder than the calculation: 0.72 against 0.43, 0.65 against 0.50, 0.64 against 0.31 (there the line itself was among the inputs of the mass and radius), and 0.35 against 0.27.
