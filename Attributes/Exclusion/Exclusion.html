Exclusion and the complete state of two electrons

Part of the series Attributes

Abstract. Exclusion needs no internal space of its own. Its mathematical object is a wave on two copies of the arguments a packet already has. For two identical packets of spin \(j\), every pure isolated-pair wave unchanged by a common rotation has full exchange sign \((-1)^{2j}\). This includes the relative position of the packets. If all pairs of one kind share one sign, and the Granularity of the joint pattern makes at least one such direction-free wave admissible for every kind, every exchange sign follows from the corresponding one-packet spin. There are zero signs to choose kind by kind. For electrons, the amplitude of two copies of any complete state then vanishes in one line. The same calculation gives the orbital-family capacities \(2,6,10,14\) and, in the published free periodic channel, a calculable resistance to compression. Three physical constructions remain to be understood: the wave of two identical patterns, why all pairs of one kind share a sign, and why a direction-free pair is admissible. The positive-exchange doublet provides an explicit test of what the existing constructions already determine.

2. Method

  1. Turn and exchange. A laboratory observer prepares two identical elementary packets and registers their frames against one triad at one clock reading. Turn one internal frame through \(2\pi\), retaining an unturned coherent reference. In a separate operation interchange the packets while keeping their frames parallel to the laboratory axes. EX-E5 and EX-E6 compare the resulting amplitudes. This tests O1–O3.
  2. Remove the directional reference. Isolate the pair, remove its centre motion and hold its separation on the laboratory ruler. Rotate the relative position and both spin arguments together. EX-E8 and EX-E9 determine every invariant wave. Restore a third centre or an additional packet label to test the boundary of O3.
  3. Read the pair. Send one packet into each input of a calibrated balanced mixer. Vary the unresolved overlap while keeping the port map and detectors fixed. EX-E2 and EX-E4 give the coincidence reading. Apply Collapse equations 55–56 to determine what an individual event preserves. This tests O2–O3.
  4. Count complete states. Put both profiles in one spatial orbital. Compare the two spin channels, the projection in EX-E11 and the action distributions in EX-E12. Then vary the length of the published flat periodic channel at fixed packet count. This gives the capacity and energy tests of O4–O5.

EX-A01. The doublet. Write the first column of \(U\in SU(2)\) as \(z=(z_1,z_2)^T\), with \(z^\dagger z=1\). At winding \(k=1\), the lowest polynomials of right character \(e^{-i\chi}\) are \(\bar z_1,\bar z_2\). They are linear harmonic polynomials in four real coordinates. 7. Completeness and completion tests

The mathematical test requires a joint-wave formula, a positive-exchange counterexample, a proof at every relative direction, the inference from one preparation to all pairs of a kind, a repeated-state projection, and reconciliation with higher one-wave action. Sections 9–13 supply all six. Section 14 gives the occupation capacities and a free-channel force in a domain with an actual spatial spectrum.

The physical completion test has three parts. Geometry must construct the wave on the two existing arguments. Its Granularity must account for one occupied exchange sector throughout a species. It must also give a nonzero pure common-rotation invariant in the isolated admissible pair space for every kind. A microscopic route through Collapse would have to produce such a supported outgoing wave. A conserved zero mean, an atomic level assignment and a finite null search each leave these tests distinct.

What would refute the universal result: one kind with independently established half-integer spin whose indistinguishable pairs crowd together with positive exchange, or one with whole-number spin whose pairs avoid each other with negative exchange. The sign must be resolved in a calibrated exchange measurement with the other distinguishing labels controlled.

The doublet in a shared scalar external state

A common turn sends the internal coefficient matrix \(C\) to \(gCg^T\). Invariance under every \(g=\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},e^{-it})\) sets the diagonal entries to zero. Invariance under the lifted half-turn

Including the relative spatial direction
