# Mixing, oscillation, and the difference between a process and its mirror-reversed partner

**Abstract.** The existing internal spaces have room for each distinction examined here. Two standing modes of the flavour interval can produce a tag that disappears and reappears. Three modes, launched with specified timings, can give different shares when preparation and detection are interchanged. A signed area on the electric charge plane can distinguish a process from its spatially reflected, charge-reversed partner. The examples put in their blends, phases and neutral-meson beat rates by hand. None of the measured values of those quantities comes out independently.

Two zeros follow exactly from the constructed motions. A freely propagating pure flavour mode has zero amplitude on every other mode. The electric circulation assigned in the companion article to distinguish a neutral kaon from its antiparticle, read with canonical momentum, never reverses under either the free electric evolution or the published charge coupling. The existing charge space nevertheless contains a continuous path to the opposite endpoint that keeps every modal energy and the total charge fixed. What prepares and detects a physical flavour blend, and what moves a neutral meson between its endpoints at its measured rate, are **not yet understood**. The first has to come from the Geometry, Granularity and Collapse of the flavour space, the second from the Geometry of the space that holds the neutral sign, or the Collapse on that space.

## 2. Method

1. **Appearance.** A source prepares tag \(\alpha\). A detector at a known laboratory distance tests tag \(\beta\). Hold their profiles and phase references fixed, vary the flight, and compare the free modal quadratures. Repeat with each tag equal to one standing mode. [MX-O1](#mx-o1) · [MX-O2](#mx-o2)
2. **Neutral conversion.** A production tag identifies one of two opposite whole-pattern endpoints. At a second vertex a charge combination tests the outgoing sign. Evolve the assigned sign under the published electric equations. Work out what two assumed route amplitudes would do on the wave. Compare a path of constant modal energy with a driven reversal whose energy exchange can be calculated. [MX-O3](#mx-o3)
3. **Conjugate and reverse.** For the conjugate comparison, reverse the particle tags, reflect spatial momenta and apparatus directions, and register the clocks and relative settings of the reflected apparatus. For the reverse comparison, interchange preparation and detection and evolve for a **positive** elapsed time. [MX-O4](#mx-o4)
4. **Strength.** Keep source normalization and detector calibration fixed while changing the flavour profile. Compare the interval integral with the reduced ratio inferred from selected yields. [MX-O5](#mx-o5)

Equal initial \(x\)-displacements and opposite \(y\)-momenta exchange opposite \(J_n\), with \(\sum J_n\) conserved by the common value-plane symmetry. ## 13. Where a conjugation-sensitive phase can live

**MX-E5.** The wave comparisons use [P02–P04](#mx-p2) and [P07–P10](#mx-p7). Their use by a transition or decay filter is conditional on [P11](#mx-p11) and [P12](#mx-p12). [MX-O4](#mx-o4)

### What two interfering routes require

For two routes to the same detected outcome, let \(A_j\ge0\), let \(\delta_j\) stay fixed in the reflected, charge-reversed preparation, and let \(\phi_j\) change sign. Then

\[
M=\sum_{j=1}^2 A_je^{i(\delta_j+\phi_j)},\qquad
\bar M=\sum_{j=1}^2 A_je^{i(\delta_j-\phi_j)}.
\]

Expanding the squared moduli cancels the two single-route terms. The difference of the two cross-term cosines gives

\[
\boxed{|M|^2-|\bar M|^2
=-4A_1A_2\sin(\delta_1-\delta_2)\sin(\phi_1-\phi_2).}
\]

A common odd phase cancels. Conjugating the entire amplitude, including its propagation phase, gives equal intensities. The real flavour multiplication integral has only phases \(0\) or \(\pi\). Flavour oscillators accumulate dynamical phases, and the companion's operation leaves flavour unchanged. Under that matched comparison the real profile supplies **exactly zero** conjugation-odd difference.

### Three tests on the actual oriented waves

Each integral uses its own space and measure. For an event using two readings, the examples assume a transition coefficient \(F_jZ_j\), where \(F_j\) is a real flavour overlap and \(Z_j\) is a separately registered, dimensionless reading. No integral of a flavour coordinate against a spin or colour coordinate occurs.

**Electric charge.** At a registered epoch encode a planar displacement by \(a=a_x+ia_y\). Then
\[
\bar a b=a\cdot b+i\det(a,b).
\]
The active reflection of both inputs preserves the dot product and reverses the determinant. Exchanging the inputs also conjugates the product. For one normalized component at every vertex, its phase telescopes around a closed sequence.

Two sine coefficients of the same electric interval suffice to retain a closed phase. Use normalized displacement profiles
\[
u=(1,0),\qquad v=(1,1)/\sqrt2,\qquad w=(1,i)/\sqrt2.
\]
Their entries are planar values on two modes. Dividing the ordinary sine-pairing integral by the profile norms gives the Hermitian pairing used here. Thus
\[
B_Q=(u^\dagger v)(v^\dagger w)(w^\dagger u)
   =\frac1{\sqrt2}\frac{1+i}{2}\frac1{\sqrt2}
   =\frac{1+i}{4}.
\]
Independent rephasings of all three profiles cancel. Reflecting the profiles or traversing the loop in reverse order gives \(B_Q^*\). Diagnostic modes with zero velocity at registration have zero modal circulation and are compatible with a neutral total.

Carry the registration into the reflected apparatus, reverse external momenta, keep an even route delay of \(\pi/2\), and use amplitudes \(1/2\) and \(iB_Q/2\). Substitution gives
\[
|1+iB_Q|^2/4=5/32,\qquad |1+iB_Q^*|^2/4=13/32.
\]
These unequal intensities follow from the **specified** profiles, strengths and delay. No observed numerical asymmetry was predicted. Indeed, taking \(v\propto(1,r)\), \(w\propto(1,s e^{i\psi})\), \(u=(1,0)\) gives a positive real multiple of \(1+rs e^{i\psi}\) for the closed product. For \(rs>1\) its argument covers the whole circle. With equal route magnitudes and even phase difference \(\pi/2\), the normalized rate difference is \(-\sin(\phi_1-\phi_2)\) and can take every value from \(-1\) to \(1\). Which reading a physical transition takes, and how the apparatus registers it under spatial reflection, are not yet understood. EQP4 reads only total charge.

**Colour.** On matched marked loops the energy pairing is
\[
D_C(a,b)=M\sum_m\omega_m^2\bar a_m b_m.
\]
For the constructed example it gives \(\sqrt3(1+i)/4\) in model energy units and normalized squared overlap \(3/8\). Marked reflection \(S\) exchanges \(a_m,a_{-m}\). Relabelling \(m\) in the sum gives
\[
D_C(Sa,Sb)=D_C(a,b),\qquad D_C(b,a)=D_C(a,b)^*.
\]
The exported complex pairing therefore has zero conjugate-rate difference under common marked reflection. Reversing only one loop changes the preparation.

There is a distinct candidate reading. Let \(d_\pm\) be the contributions from the two directions to the normalized pairing. Then
\[
Z_C=(|d_+|^2+|d_-|^2)+i(|d_+|^2-|d_-|^2).
\]
Reflection exchanges \(d_+,d_-\), so it conjugates \(Z_C\). Exchanging the two input profiles conjugates each \(d_\pm\), leaving \(Z_C\) unchanged. Taking this directional contrast as a transition quadrature is an assumption of the example. A lepton application would also require an independently identified colour preparation.

**Spin.** On the constructed doublet,
\[
D_S(c,d)=\int\bar\Phi_c\Phi_d\,d\mu=c^\dagger d.
\]
It can be complex. Common proper rotations preserve it. The companion acts as identity on spin. Transport that keeps the spin coefficients fixed under the external reflection gives equal conjugate intensities. The published rotor reads a real scalar proportional to \(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S\).

Algebraically \(Jc=i\sigma_y\bar c\) gives \(D_S(Jc,Jd)=D_S(c,d)^*\). It also obeys \(J^2=-I\) and reverses the spin vector. No construction identifies this map with forward-time particle conjugation and parity for the actual preparation. Using the massive spin-half packet for a spin-zero meson would change the system.

**Flavour timing supplies a further test on one space.** For positive occupied mode indices \(m,n\), put \(T_{mn}=z_m^{\,n}\bar z_n^{\,m}\). E1 gives

### How many modes make a phase physical?

On a tree of amplitude comparisons, start at any vertex and choose successive vertex phases to make each link real. On a closed path the endpoint rephasings cancel from the product. Two distinct routes between the same endpoints already supply a relative phase. A graph with one Hermitian link per unordered pair needs three vertices for its first nontrivial simple cycle.

For one **closed orthonormal tag table** put \(b_m=U_{\beta m}U_{\alpha m}^*\). With two modes and distinct tags, orthogonality gives \(b_1+b_2=0\), hence \(\operatorname{Im}(b_1b_2^*)=0\). The count in E2 gives zero irreducible phases for two tags and one for three.

Three modes suffice on **flavour alone**. Index successive lines by \(m=0,1,2\) with frequencies \((m+1)\omega_F\). Take
\[
U_{\alpha m}=3^{-1/2}e^{2\pi i\alpha m/3}.
\]
The geometric sum makes the rows orthonormal. The real flavour wave can realize any such quadratures: \(q_n=\sqrt{2/\mathcal I}\,\Re z_n/\omega_n\) and \(\dot q_n=\sqrt{2/\mathcal I}\,\Im z_n\). With the convention of E1 the reciprocal source launches the conjugate row. Equal modal magnitudes and these registered launch timings supply the whole example without a charge-plane direction for each flavour mode.

At \(\omega_F\tau=2\pi/3\), the three contributions to \(0\to1\) have equal phase. Those to \(1\to0\) sum to zero. Therefore
\[
P_{0\to1}=1,\qquad P_{1\to0}=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Im}(b_0b_1^*)=-\sqrt3/18\quad(0\to1).
\]
This establishes the three-mode minimum for this class of tables. The companion leaves these flavour timings unchanged under particle conjugation. Supplying the same coefficients as directions on the electric plane would let its reflection conjugate them, and would require a physical correspondence between each flavour mode and a charge direction. No construction supplies that correspondence. The conventional claim about three physical families is **IMPORT**, comparison only.

### Does the same phase enter the reverse process?

With reciprocal preparation and detection, interchanging the tags sends \(b_m\) to \(b_m^*\) at the same positive flight time. Expansion gives
\[
P_{\alpha\to\beta}-P_{\beta\to\alpha}
=4\sum_{m<n}\operatorname{Im}(b_m b_n^*)\sin[(\omega_m-\omega_n)\tau].
\]
If the reflected, charge-reversed apparatus also conjugates \(b_m\) and keeps the propagation phases fixed, it gives the same difference for **every** such table. This is an identity of that registration and does not calibrate its entries.

A neutral pair illustrates a separate **two-propagation-mode** result. In its signed basis take
\[

\begin{pmatrix}
c_t&-i e^{-i\varphi}s_t\\
-i e^{i\varphi}s_t&c_t
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
c_t=\cos(x/2),\quad s_t=\sin(x/2),\quad x=\Delta\nu t .
\]
A common phase has cancelled. Add the registered decay filter \(f=(h+\bar h)/\sqrt2\). It is invariant under exchanging the sign tags in this preparation. Multiplication gives
\[
P_{h\to f}=\tfrac12(1+\sin x\sin\varphi),\qquad
P_{\bar h\to f}=P_{f\to h}=\tfrac12(1-\sin x\sin\varphi).
\]
The invariant is the phase of the off-diagonal transfer coefficient times \(\langle f|\bar h\rangle/\langle f|h\rangle\). Endpoint rephasings cancel. The charge-tag and genuinely interchanged-filter differences both equal \(\sin x\sin\varphi\). The pure sign-to-sign shares are equal. The third **filter preparation** is what distinguishes this experiment from a closed two-tag table.

|G_{21}|^2-|G_{12}|^2=2\operatorname{Im}(m g^*).

Here \(m,g\) have inverse-time units and describe dispersive coupling and common-channel loss. A passive realization needs a positive semidefinite loss matrix. The filtered \(B\) difference, the pure-sign kaon comparison and the charged direct-decay difference can measure different physical phase combinations.

## 18. What is not yet understood

- **Which modes and how many tags occur.** Flavour Granularity must identify the physical band and line indices independently of the measured slopes.
- **What prepares each blend and detects it with its measured count share.** Flavour Geometry, Granularity and Collapse must give the source profile, detector quadratures, permitted outcomes and sampling.
- **Which energy travels with the pattern.** The assembly of a whole pattern in Geometry §14 must identify each contribution to \(E_*\) and relate its clock to the measured centre motion.
- **Where the tagged neutral sign is held.** The Geometry and Granularity of the candidate charge or colour space must give a sign reading testable independently of the hadron name.
- **How the neutral sign changes while charge stays fixed.** The Geometry of the space carrying the sign, or Collapse on that space with its time-dependent outcome statistics, must give the distinct whole-pattern rates.
- **Which reading a transition takes for a reflected or reversed difference.** The weak mechanism in Geometry §16 must give the registration, the action on flavour timings and the decay filters.
- **What sets the family-changing profile.** The same weak mechanism must give its modal strengths and channel responses before the measured reduced ratio can anchor a coefficient.
- **What supplies loss, survival and final-channel counts.** Collapse on the participating spaces must connect a tagged whole pattern to the complete measured products and their efficiencies.
