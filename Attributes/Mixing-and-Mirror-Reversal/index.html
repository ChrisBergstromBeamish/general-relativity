Mixing, oscillation, and the difference between a process and its mirror-reversed partner

Part of the series Attributes

Abstract. The existing internal spaces have room for each distinction examined here. Two standing modes of the flavour interval can produce a tag that disappears and reappears. Three modes, launched with specified timings, can give different shares when preparation and detection are interchanged. A signed area on the electric charge plane can distinguish a process from its spatially reflected, charge-reversed partner. The examples put in their blends, phases and neutral-meson beat rates by hand. None of the measured values of those quantities comes out independently.

Two zeros follow exactly from the constructed motions. A freely propagating pure flavour mode has zero amplitude on every other mode. The electric circulation assigned in the companion article to distinguish a neutral kaon from its antiparticle, read with canonical momentum, never reverses under either the free electric evolution or the published charge coupling. The existing charge space nevertheless contains a continuous path to the opposite endpoint that keeps every modal energy and the total charge fixed. What prepares and detects a physical flavour blend, and what moves a neutral meson between its endpoints at its measured rate, are not yet understood. The first has to come from the Geometry, Granularity and Collapse of the flavour space, the second from the Geometry of the space that holds the neutral sign, or the Collapse on that space.

On a tree of amplitude comparisons, start at any vertex and choose successive vertex phases to make each link real. On a closed path the endpoint rephasings cancel from the product. \[ P_{\alpha\to\beta}-P_{\beta\to\alpha} =4\sum_{m<n}\operatorname{Im}(b_m b_n^*)\sin[(\omega_m-\omega_n)\tau]. \]
