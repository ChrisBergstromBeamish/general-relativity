# Magnetism: what turns when a spinning charge passes moving charges?

**Abstract.** The magnetic observations considered here require no internal space of their own. A charged centre bends, a prepared spin turns, and a suspended magnet transfers angular momentum to its support. Each measurement distinguishes responses of quantities already present in the construction. As built, no construction exerts a force on a centre through its charge, even from a source at rest, and the frames’ own carriage of the rest axes turns the spin by \(1-\gamma\) per turn of the velocity, zero at low speed. On one spin doublet, rotation fixes the direction of a weak magnetic moment and leaves one signed coefficient. The electron trap measures that coefficient. The constructions do not give its value or the factor two. The charge construction supplies a separate result for neutral matter. One whole wave with exactly zero total charge can have a positive, negative or zero reading when its charge density is sampled unevenly. In iron, joint rotation gives an angular-momentum balance. If the rod receives the entire axial transfer and all changing moments have one spin slope, its mechanical ratio equals the one-packet ratio exactly.

### Domain and assumptions

The single-packet calculation is local, weak in source excitation and linear in the dipole setting. The exact speed-dependent comparisons concern planar transverse bends in a flat laboratory. The trap coefficient is the low-speed limit. Silver spot spacing, iron domains and the conversion of storage-ring counts to a free-muon moment require their own apparatus or material constructions. Maxwell’s equations, the Dirac equation, spin-statistics and a constituent model of the proton supply no step of the magnetic derivation.

### The two measured moments as equations on one interval

Expand the bounded sampling profile in the interval cosine basis as \(w(x)=w_0+\sum_{k\ge1}w_k\cos(kx)\), with \(w_0\) its mean. Orthogonality gives, in reference-action units,

\qquad

The sums also follow by integrating the bounded profile against the mean density. The constant part samples the net charge. It drops out for the neutron and contributes \(w_0\) for the proton.

\[

\frac{\Lambda}{2}\,\overline W_p=\mu_p,\qquad \overline W_p\ne0.}

\[

\qquad

One factor \(1/2\) comes from the Fourier integral and the other from \(S_\parallel/s_0\). Equivalently the branch calibration is \(\Lambda_+=\Lambda/2\).

The proton wave is a physical unknown as well. The positive example in the colour construction is one wave on the electric interval. Its \(m=1,2,3\) lines have squared amplitudes \(2/3,1/3,1/9\) and senses \(+,+,-\). Hence their circulations are \(2/3,2/3,-1/3\), their energies sum to \(3\), and

### What the suspended iron inherits

Let \(\mathbf L\) generate rotation of the body, including its attached axes, and let \(\mathbf S_i\) have the brackets in A5. Then \(\mathbf G=\mathbf L+\sum_i\mathbf S_i\) generates a simultaneous rotation of the body and all spins. Indeed \(\delta Z=\{Z,\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf G\}\) rotates each of these variables by the same infinitesimal angle \(\boldsymbol\theta\). If their interaction energy is invariant under that joint turn, then for all \(\boldsymbol\theta\)
\[
0=\delta H=\{H,\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf G\},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \dot{\mathbf G}=\{\mathbf G,H\}=0 .
\]
A5’s \(-\Gamma_i\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf S_i\) has this invariance for every packet, including when several axes belong to one body. For an applied axial setting \(B(t)\hat{\mathbf z}\), the extra torque is \(\sum_i\gamma_i\mathbf S_i\times B(t)\hat{\mathbf z}\). Its axial component is identically zero. This establishes the axial balance even while the setting changes. It makes no corresponding assertion for a transverse component in the driven experiment.

Include external angular impulse and other recipients as in A10. For a spin-only reversal with corrected \(J_{\rm ext}-\Delta J_{\rm other}=0\) and one common signed slope \(\gamma_s\),
\[
\Delta J_{\rm rod}=-\sum_i\Delta S_i,\qquad
\Delta\mu=\gamma_s\sum_i\Delta S_i,\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\Delta\mu}{\Delta J_{\rm rod}}=-\gamma_s.}
\]
The last ratio requires nonzero net transfer. It is independent of the number of packets and the degree of reversal. A pure electron-spin transfer predicts the mechanical factor \(\lvert g_e\rvert\), including its excess over two, from the separate trap measurement. It introduces no second gyromagnetic constant. If another recipient takes a fraction \(b\) of the spin transfer, the ratio becomes \(-\gamma_s/(1-b)\). Rotational symmetry alone cannot identify the recipient or establish a common slope.

The material departure has a definite *conditional* interpretation. Suppose electron spin and external orbital motion alone change, their axial angular changes have the same sense, the rod receives their sum, and P7 gives \(g_L=1\). Write \(x=\Delta L/(\Delta S+\Delta L)\). In units \(e/(2M_e)\), the mechanical magnitude and the orbital fraction of the changed moment are
\[
G_{\rm rod}=(1-x)g_s+x,\qquad
x=\frac{g_s-G_{\rm rod}}{g_s-1},\qquad
\frac{\lvert\Delta\mu_L\rvert}{\lvert\Delta\mu\rvert}=\frac{x}{G_{\rm rod}} .
\]
For the numerical example \(G_{\rm rod}=1.919\), \(g_s=2.00231930\), these are \(8.3127\%\) of the reversed angular momentum and \(4.3318\%\) of the changed moment. Here one supplied mechanical ratio determines one fraction. The approximately \(4.2\%\) departure of this example from the spin-only value is not predicted. A separate measurement of the orbital transfer would test the interpretation.

### What determines the sign in a material

At fixed volume \(\mathcal V\), temperature, composition and shape, let \(B\) be the applied setting along a fixed axis. For \(\mu_h=-E'_h(B)\), differentiating the sample magnetization gives
\[
\chi_B=\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal M}{\partial B}\right|_0
=\frac1{\mathcal V}\sum_h[p'_h(0)\mu_h(0)-p_h(0)E''_h(0)].
\]
Conversion to a susceptibility quoted against the applied setting \(H\) requires calibration of the setting inside the specimen, which depends on its shape. The first term measures redistribution among pre-existing moments. The second measures a change of the moment of an occupied state, including induced charge circulation where its dynamics produces it.

Take \(E_\pm=E_0\mp mB+kB^2/2\) and \(p_\pm=(1\pm hB)/2\) near zero. Then \(\mathcal V\chi_B=mh-k\). For \(m=h=1\), \(k=0\) gives \(+1\) and \(k=2\) gives \(-1\) at the same zero-setting moments. Thus both signs can be expressed on existing pattern readings. These numbers specify examples. Neither example predicts the susceptibility of a named specimen.

A material calculation requires joint occupations, interactions between patterns and the resulting populations and energies. Antisymmetric exchange, if used, excludes duplicate complete states by A12. It gives neither iron’s alignment energy nor the curvature of an occupied orbital energy. Paramagnetic redistribution and diamagnetic induction can both contribute in one specimen.

## 16. What is not yet understood

- **Static centre force.** The Geometry of the charge-coupling space must produce a comparison that depends on external separation from a resting charge.
- **Moving and changing sources.** The Geometry of charge coupling must connect the comparison angle between frames and calculate source motion, multiple-source response and reciprocal momentum transfer.
- **Magnetic spin rate.** The Geometry of the spin space and its comparison with the charge-coupling response must determine how the source turns the rest axes and on which particle preparations that response holds.
- **Lepton excess and electric slope.** The same spin–source construction must account for the species-dependent residual and determine the electric spin difference after the magnetic response is present.
- **Whole hadron preparations.** Charge Geometry must supply the physical profiles. Granularity and Collapse on the relevant existing spaces must explain which whole proton and neutron preparations persist or are produced.
- **Neutron orientation.** The hadron construction must relate a separately observable signed reading to the reflection-odd charge density. The proposed baryon reading has yet to establish that correspondence.
- **Hadron moment scale.** Charge-coupling Geometry must derive how the source samples the density, how the reading enters the spin energy, and its strength in proton-reference units.
- **Silver spot spacing.** Atomic spin and charge-coupling Geometry must give the silver slope. The analyzer Geometry and its Collapse readout must connect the prepared channels to measured positions.
- **Iron transfer and domains.** The interacting spin and external orbital geometries must identify actual angular-momentum recipients and slopes. The specimen’s available patterns and their changes require its Granularity and Collapse construction.
- **Material susceptibility.** The joint occupations of existing electron patterns, their interactions and their change with temperature and setting must determine \(p'_h\) and \(E''_h\). Antisymmetric exchange alone fixes neither.
