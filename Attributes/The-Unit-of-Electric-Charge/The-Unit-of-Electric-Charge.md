# The unit of electric charge

*Attributes · 27 November 2026*

**Abstract.** Rotate a charge wave and its momentum together through one turn in the wave’s oriented plane. For every finite-energy preparation, the action around that turn is exactly \(2\pi J\), where \(J\) is the signed circulation. If the action phase returns around this turn for every complete pattern, then \(J=m\hbar\), with \(m\in\mathbb Z\). One electric calibration then gives one step for every family, whatever the interval length, wave speed, action coefficient, mode or polarization. No free complete pattern can carry a fraction of the step. Components of one pattern can carry any real shares consistent with its total action.

The phase comparison on the electric turn is the same mathematical step that the published spin Granularity construction takes on its phase circle. What makes this phase close physically is not yet understood on either space; for charge, the Granularity of the charge space has to give it from its Geometry. On the charge wave’s other cycle, the common oscillator phase, closing the phase in the same action unit fixes the ordinary action and leaves charge continuous. No additional internal space is shown to be needed for the unit. Charge balance in measured events supplies a further constraint. The Collapse of the charge space has to explain that balance on each outcome.

**CU-A02 · From instrument response to charge.** In the terminal-mobility reduction, let \(d\) be the marked drop distance, \(b\) the drag coefficient, \(W\) the effective downward pull of gravity on the drop and \(\mathcal E\) the applied setting. With \(v_f=d/t_f\) downward when off and \(v_r=d/t_r\) upward when on,

\[
bv_f=W,\qquad bv_r=q\mathcal E-W,\qquad
q=\frac{bd}{\mathcal E}\left(\frac1{t_f}+\frac1{t_r}\right).
\]

The same drop shares its mobility across changes. Its absolute reading in coulombs also needs calibration.

For an un-ionized molecule containing \(Z\) protons, \(Z\) electrons and \(N\) neutrons, the additive reduction gives

\[
Q_{\rm mol}=Z\delta+Nq_n,\qquad \delta=q_p+q_e .
\]

For \(^{32}\mathrm S{}^{19}\mathrm F_6\), \(Z=16+6(9)=70\) and \(N=16+6(10)=76\). One gas gives one linear combination. An independent neutron measurement supplies a bound on the second term. Substituting \(q_n=\delta\) instead gives \(Q_{\rm mol}=146\delta\). If that substitution is inferred from neutron decay, the resulting bound already contains an event-balance assumption. The empirical precision travels with these **IMPORT** reductions. [CU-P02](#cu-p02)

## What is not yet understood

- **Why the phase closes on the whole electric turn.** Charge Granularity has to derive this from charge Geometry. Spin Granularity reaches the same physical question at C-LIFT.
- **Which entities emerging from an electric event are complete products.** Charge Collapse has to construct their wave data and separation.
- **Why total charge balances on each physical branch and how charge passes between complete patterns.** Charge Collapse has to give the changes of all participants, including sources and receivers.
- **Why the electron occupies \(-1\) and the proton \(+1\).** Reconstruction of each independently tagged whole has to give its observed sector.
- **Whether the phase also closes on the common oscillator cycle.** Charge Granularity has to determine this. The charge-step measurements distinguish the signed turn but do not settle a second closing.
- **Whether individual modes have a physical step or the proton occupies the two-half spectrum.** Charge Granularity and reconstruction of the proton have to determine the modal data.
- **Whether a persistent tilt between two separate spaces reads charge.** Geometry has to supply the registered directions, their relative comparison and its response.
- **How the in-sample fractional transport and the dimensionless electric strength are read from the constructed waves.** The relevant charge and coupling preparations need calibrated response calculations.
